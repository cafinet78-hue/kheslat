# The Bridge That Froze the Thames

Original short in the cartoon-3d-short style. Composition `FrostShort` (planned), 1080x1920, 30 fps, target 60-65 s.

## Facts (with sources)
1. Frost fairs were held on the frozen Thames from the early 1600s to 1814, during the Little Ice Age.
   [Museum of London](https://www.museumoflondon.org.uk/discover/frost-fairs), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames_frost_fairs)
2. The 1814 fair began on 1 February 1814 and lasted four days, on the ice between Blackfriars Bridge and London Bridge. It was the last one.
   [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames_frost_fairs), [Regency History](https://www.regencyhistory.net/2020/05/the-frost-fair-of-1814.html)
3. An elephant was led across the river below Blackfriars Bridge during the 1814 fair.
   [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames_frost_fairs)
4. Printers set up presses on the ice. Souvenirs read "Printed on the River Thames", and a visitor's name could be set in type and printed for a few pence.
   [Crouch Rare Books](https://crouchrarebooks.com/?p=34474), [Museum of London](https://www.museumoflondon.org.uk/discover/frost-fairs)
5. A sheep was roasted on the ice. People paid sixpence to watch, then the meat sold at a shilling a slice as "Lapland mutton".
   [Past Tense](https://pasttense.co.uk/2016/02/01/today-in-londons-rowdy-history-the-last-frost-fair-opens-on-the-thames-1814/), [Regency History](https://www.regencyhistory.net/2020/05/the-frost-fair-of-1814.html)
6. When the thaw came, booths and printing presses were carried off downstream, and a diary records the ice sweeping about forty barges through the arches of Blackfriars Bridge.
   [Regency History](https://www.regencyhistory.net/2020/05/the-frost-fair-of-1814.html), [Crouch Rare Books](https://crouchrarebooks.com/?p=30412)
7. Old London Bridge stood on 19 narrow arches with wide pier footings (starlings). It worked almost like a dam: the water above it was slow and froze more easily.
   [History Today](https://www.historytoday.com/archive/feature/great-frost-fair-1683-4), [Exploring London](https://exploring-london.com/tag/old-london-bridge/)
8. New London Bridge opened on 1 August 1831 with five wide arches, and the old bridge was pulled down. Later the embankments narrowed and sped up the river. The Thames has not frozen over in central London since 1814. (A milder climate also helped.)
   [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Thames_frost_fairs), [Londonist](https://londonist.com/london/best-of-london/could-the-thames-ever-freeze-over-again)

Analogy: a puddle freezes overnight, while the stream next to it keeps running. Still water freezes; moving water keeps mixing and doesn't.

## Script (voice-over, one steady tone)
**Hook**
In 1814, Londoners walked an elephant across the River Thames. No bridge. No boat. Just ice.

**Setting**
That February, London got so cold the river froze from bank to bank. So Londoners did the sensible thing. They threw a party on it.

**Action**
First came the skaters. Then tents selling gin. Then swings, skittles, and printing presses that printed your name on a card for a few pence.
Someone roasted a whole sheep on the ice, charged sixpence just to watch it, then sold it at a shilling a slice.
And then, just below Blackfriars Bridge, came the elephant.

**Snap**
Four days later, the thaw arrived. The ice split, and tents, printing presses and about forty barges went sailing down the river.

**Deadpan double**
Luckily, the elephant was already on the other side.
Unluckily, the printing presses were not.

**Explanation**
But the real reason the river froze was a bridge. Old London Bridge stood on nineteen narrow arches, and it worked almost like a dam. Above it, the water barely moved.
And still water freezes much faster than moving water. It's why a puddle freezes overnight, while the stream right next to it keeps on running.

**Consequence**
In 1831, London opened a new bridge with just five wide arches, and pulled the old one down. The river sped up. And the Thames has not frozen over in London since.

**Callback twist**
So the greatest party ever held on the Thames wasn't stopped by the weather.
It was stopped by a bridge.

## Visual plan (no captions or labels)
- Winter London: snow on roofs and banks, crisp blue day sky; golden light for the ending.
- The frozen Thames as a painted ice sheet with snow drifts and skate scratches; old London Bridge (19 arches, starlings) in the distance.
- Cast: Londoners in winter coats, top hats and bonnets; skaters; a printer at his press; a cook at the sheep spit; the elephant with its keeper.
- Thaw: the ice cracks into floes, tents tilt and drift, barges spin through the arches.
- Explanation: water squeezing through narrow arches and going still behind them; a kid next to a frozen puddle and a running stream.
- Consequence: the new five-arch bridge; the old one coming down; the river running fast.
