# Persian narration rule (user's standing instruction, applies to EVERY video)

Before any Persian voice-over is recorded or sent to TTS, act as a professional Persian narration editor and
ElevenLabs V4 specialist and rewrite the script:
- Correct, natural, colloquial modern Persian (محاوره‌ای و خودمانی), never bookish, literary, formal or translated-sounding.
- Short, tidy, easy-to-read sentences; split long ones; replace words that are hard to say; natural Persian word order;
  fix verbs, pronouns and grammar; no needless repetition or filler. Use "هست/است" instead of "می‌باشد".
- ElevenLabs V4: standard punctuation for natural pauses (comma, period, question mark, ellipsis only where they belong);
  avoid spellings the TTS may mispronounce; audio tags such as [laughs], [whispers], [sighs] only when they truly fit, sparingly.
- Keep the author's meaning, facts and order; do not drop sentences unless purely redundant; do not lengthen.
- Final check as a voice actor: no tongue-twisters, natural aloud, complete sentences, pauses in the right place,
  understood on one listen, low risk of TTS mispronunciation.
- Output: only the final edited text, no notes on the edits, no emoji, no summary; keep section/dialogue order and speaker names.
Golden rule: it should sound like a well-spoken Persian speaker talking to the viewer, not reading from paper.
